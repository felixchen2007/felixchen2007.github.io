Embedded systems

Weather Monitor PCB

I independently designed an ESP32-based PCB in Altium Designer to monitor temperature, humidity, and solar power. The system displays live measurements on a 240 × 240 ST7789 TFT screen and a custom webpage.

Firmware

I developed firmware for the DHT11 and INA219 sensors to read temperature, humidity, voltage, current, and power. I also debugged I²C addressing, sensor communication, and pin assignments during hardware integration.

Development

The project progressed from a breadboard prototype to an intermediate PCB using an ESP32 development board, then to a fully integrated custom ESP32 PCB. Each stage brought the sensing, display, and monitoring functions into a more integrated design.

Adjustable Buck-Boost Converter

I independently designed, manufactured, and assembled a four-layer adjustable buck-boost converter PCB around the Texas Instruments TPS63000. The Altium Designer layout includes feedback, enable, input and output filtering, and voltage-selection circuitry.

PCB Design

I laid out dedicated ground and power planes, kept high-current paths short, and considered component placement, copper pours, and thermal behavior. I also imported schematic symbols, footprints, and 3D models from LCSC and EasyEDA, checking package and pin compatibility.

Design verification

I checked the board against design rules and added test points for hardware validation. Before hardware testing, I developed a simplified Simulink model to explore transient behavior and control concepts.

Systems modeling

Rainwater Harvester

Developed an Excel simulation of a rainwater harvesting and treatment system for a two-person, off-grid household in a remote BC community as part of a six-person team. Modelled rainfall, water demand, tank storage, and energy use, accounting for collection, pumping, filtration, disinfection, and power requirements.

Used pump and system curves to determine flow rates and compare tank sizes, piping, treatment, and power options. The simulation guided component selection by weighing cost, reliability, and maintenance needs.

Arduino Claw

Built and programmed an Arduino-controlled claw as part of a five-person team, combining motors, sensors, and mechanical components. Developed the control logic to read sensor inputs and operate the claw.

Assembled and wired the prototype, tested its response, and resolved hardware and software issues throughout development. Earned top studio scores in a three-round competition involving varied objects, timed foam-block collection, and working with other teams to lift larger objects.

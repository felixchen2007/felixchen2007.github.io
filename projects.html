Embedded systems

Weather monitor PCB

I designed an ESP32-based PCB in Altium Designer to monitor temperature, humidity, and solar power. The system displays live measurements on a 240 × 240 ST7789 TFT screen and a custom webpage.

My contribution

I developed firmware for the DHT11 and INA219 sensors to read temperature, humidity, voltage, current, and power. I also debugged I²C addressing, sensor communication, and pin assignments during hardware integration.

Development

The project progressed from a breadboard prototype to an intermediate PCB using an ESP32 development board, then to a fully integrated custom ESP32 PCB. Each stage brought the sensing, display, and monitoring functions into a more integrated design.

Adjustable buck-boost converter

I designed, manufactured, and assembled a four-layer adjustable buck-boost converter PCB around the Texas Instruments TPS63000. The Altium Designer layout includes feedback, enable, input and output filtering, and voltage-selection circuitry.

My contribution

I laid out dedicated ground and power planes, kept high-current paths short, and considered component placement, copper pours, and thermal behavior. I also imported schematic symbols, footprints, and 3D models from LCSC and EasyEDA, checking package and pin compatibility.

Design verification

I checked the board against design rules and added test points for hardware validation. Before hardware testing, I developed a simplified Simulink model to explore transient behavior and control concepts.

Systems modeling

Rainwater Harvester

Developed an Excel-based model for a rainwater harvesting system serving a remote two-person household. The model considered water demand, rainfall, storage, pump behavior, and system-level tradeoffs.

My contribution

I organized assumptions, built spreadsheet calculations, interpreted pump and system behavior, and communicated results through visuals and presentation slides. Excel calculations helped compare design choices for water availability, reliability, cost, and maintainability.

What I learned

The design could not be judged by a single number. Balancing technical performance with stakeholder needs helped me understand how connected constraints shape an engineering decision.

Arduino claw

Built and programmed an Arduino-controlled claw system using electrical components, wiring, and microcontroller logic. The goal was to control a physical mechanism through programmed input and output, and make its response reliable during testing.

My contribution

I connected the Arduino to the components, organized the wiring, debugged input and output behavior, and adjusted the code to make the mechanism respond as intended.

What I learned

A weak connection or small logic mistake could change the entire system response. Troubleshooting taught me to slow down, isolate problems, and verify each part of the system step by step.
